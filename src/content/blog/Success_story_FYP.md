I'm actually very proud of the title so please be proud of it too.


This is a story about a girl’s bachelor’s Final Year Project and how it went from being a mess to getting an award (read to the end to know what award it is).


First and foremost, background information (nobody asked for): I recently finished my degree and am awaiting graduation right now. Let me tell you the painful story of my Final Year Project titled Brain-Computer Interface For Wheelchair Control.


It all started in Mid-2025 during the title selection process. There were hundreds of titles and supervisors to choose from. I went through each and every one of them, deciding if they are interesting enough, asking AI to elaborate more, taking notes, and even researching similar projects on YouTube. It was a long process, took me hours but then, when I reached the very last supervisor (Dr. Zaizu) on the list and saw the title “Brain-computer interface for wheelchair control” my heart skipped a beat. It was like when Spider-Man’s spidey senses were tingling: the whole world slowed, my vision blurred around the edges, my heart rate went up, everything else was just noise at that moment.


The moment I read that title, everything else was scrapped. Don’t get me wrong, I had my doubts. It took me a few hours to decide if the difficulty and satisfaction ratio was worth it, but then I told myself, the longer I wait, the smaller my opportunity window becomes (as it was a first come first serve basis, with respect to each student’s CGPA). I kept trying to gaslight myself that I was a great student (I had reasons to believe so). My project completion rate was 100% (not including scope depth), and I'm definitely a person with grit, who has persevered in so many scenarios, even my diploma FYP and intern (a story for another time guys). 


So it took me maybe 5 hours to contemplate, then I just applied for that title immediately. Again, I kept gaslighting myself since then because I knew it was going to be super challenging but I just kept telling myself I could do it. I knew I could, deep down in my heart I knew. Until the very last day when it mattered (my exhibition and viva) so i think my mindset played a 50% role in my success.


When I returned to my campus, everything began, but there were so many error messages and the learning curve was so steep looking back. One of the reasons why was because the headset given to me by my supervisor was 10 years old and it was in a very bad condition (story for another time again) but to give you the general picture, i had to scrub it using vinegar and alcohol, tape it using teflon tape (used for water pipes), and use dental bands just to hold the components in place. It was very bad, and that might be an understatement. 


Generally, I’m not an anxious person but I felt the anxiety creeping up every night, every supervisor meeting, and every time I looked at my prototype. I overcame this painfully slowly by repeating “there is still enough time” over and over again every time the anxiety crept in. It did make me calmer, however it made me procrastinate a lot. In the future, I would still use this technique but I would make sure the work is still rolling.


The plot thickens during my mid-semester break. We were told that our exhibition date was pushed up by 2 weeks, so instead of 1 month left of time, I had 2 weeks. You can imagine the horror and how my heart dropped because at that moment, nothing had progressed much for my project. I literally felt like throwing up. 


So I took deep breaths to calm myself down, and I decided not to go back home for the mid-semester break. I was alone for that whole week. However,in that week everything changed. My progress skyrocketed. It was like the learning curve spiked up after months of being almost horizontally linear. I’d like to say that my sacrifice of not going back home was worth it. I learned delayed gratification in that one week.


The day came, the dreaded exhibition, and we found out that the tables were incredibly small. To top it off, I got the closed end (red box) of the booth as shown below.

![A picture of my setup layout location during exhibition](/layoutexpofyp.png)

Because nobody was coming into my booth, I had to bring my A2-sized laminated poster and wheelchair prototype outside to pitch to the present lecturers, students, and industry panels. I learned then that we need to improvise in any situation to get any engagement, traction or attention after being dealt our hand. After that, everything went well, and I never expect to win.. drumroll please.. Industry’s Choice Award.. Actually, I did expect a gold award but not the top 5. 


Please don’t get me wrong, I prayed really hard and conditioned my mind to expect an award so it wasn't really a shock because I know I did well, I was confident in my project, and I believed in my heart that I would win something. I guess it was the law of attraction working in my favour.


All in all, I am so grateful for all my hard work and sacrifices. My prayers and mindset, the help given and received, all the research done, and countless sleepless nights. I learned that grit takes me further than current knowledge, and it can help with gaining knowledge. I also learned that we must win in our minds first before winning in real life.
