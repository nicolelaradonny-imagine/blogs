Okay you might be wondering: Why did I suddenly become an ambassador of silent walking? Who am I to tell people to go on silent walks? And remove the pleasure of music or knowledge of podcasts? 

Frankly, I'm the last qualified person to be writing this, but it did change my life, so i’m just sharing my thoughts. My life is a whole lot calmer, I feel much happier, and I'm really (hear me out) addicted to it actually. So without further adieu, here are five reasons why I think everyone on this beautiful green spinning planet needs to go walking without listening to anything (apart from nature).

Let’s start with the most prominent effect on this list, silent walking is very very very good in managing stress. I started silently walking in my final semester of university, and I was going through a tough time. My final year project was not going well and I had an issue with the university about my safety. So I was super stressed. However, silent walking 45min a day had me feeling calm, and grateful for all the challenges thrown my way. 

After years of being on a self-improvement journey, there’s a ton of research and podcasts out there that prove we need less stimulation in a world full of mobile games and social media. At first, I was like: enough with this nonsense (I know, been there done that), but after I tried it for a month... Man I hate to be the one to tell you but the effects are incredible. We as human beings need more silence (aka boredom) for our innermost thoughts (or fears, or demons) to form and show itself, as well as our creative process to imagine. When did we stop imagining our future? Our lives? I’ll tell you when, since the rise of phones and social media. Society nowadays has too many dopamine spikes so we need to reset our dopamine baseline by silently walking.

The third reason is that it helps with body movement. My movement goal is 10k steps everyday and silent walking makes it very achievable. You don't have to do 10k steps if just starting out, doing 5k is enough for beginners. Hell, even 1k (15 minutes) is enough, what matters is that you start, and stick with it for a week.  So in that sense it kinda is a two birds one stone situation. As i have summed up in the equation below:

##### Clearing my mind + moving my body = Happier Lara

I admit, I do not have the best temper. I used to find it so easy to be angry at my loved ones. However, after a few weeks of silent walking, even my loved ones notice that I haven't lost control of my temper recently, so it makes me happy and also clear-headed about everything. I am able to think much clearer (go through all the possibilities in every situation yet still ground myself down and not overthink). I advocate for silent walking because it bandages the broken cracks of my relationships with my loved ones, and I will forever be grateful for it.

Lastly, I always do 10 fingers of gratitude while on walks. I try to do specific things like that short conversation earlier in the day, or the lunch I ate that day. It makes me grateful and happy. Gratitude always makes us feel good about ourselves and our life. To be frank, I haven't been silently walking every day for the last 2 weeks since I’m back in my hometown, and no wonder I feel much more jittery, easily triggered, and I snap more easily at my loved ones. I'm not loving it right now honestly. But I do try to go for walks at least 3 times a week and I feel much lighter and calmer whenever I do so.

So there you go, five unqualified and unsolicited opinions as to why I make such a claim. Overall I am a happier, calmer, stress-free individual after silent walking for a few months. I’m able to think clearer, be more grateful, and complain less in these few months. I ask you who are reading this to go for a 10-min walk today without listening to anything and see if it makes you feel better. It will be hard at first, because we as humans are so used to being stimulated and distracted every second of the day so boredom feels alien to us. But just take that leap of faith, and watch your world change.
